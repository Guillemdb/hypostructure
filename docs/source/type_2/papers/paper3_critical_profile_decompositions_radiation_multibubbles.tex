\title[Local Multibubble Exclusion]{Local Multibubble and Cascade Exclusion for Type II Navier--Stokes Blowup}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\begin{abstract}
We formulate the multibubble and cascade part of the local CKN Type II
exclusion argument.  The starting point is a positive local CKN concentration
sequence at a singular point, not a nonlocal critical data sequence.  Failure of
compact-cylinder suitable compactness, splitting of retained CKN mass, strict
same-point scale cascades, separated active points, and localized gauge
degeneracy are handled by local decoupling and scale-rigidity theorems.
Together with the local Type I tangent criterion and the local single-core
criterion, these theorems exclude multibubble and cascade Type II cases.
\end{abstract}
\maketitle

\section{Local scenarios}

Let \((u_n,p_n)\) be parabolic blow-ups around a singular point with positive
local CKN density.  On \(Q_R=B_R\times(-R^2,0)\), use the local suitable
quantities \(A(R),E(R),C(R),D(R)\).  If \(A+E+C+D\) is bounded on every compact
cylinder and a single nondegenerate localized core is selected, the case is
single-core.  The complementary local cases are compactness failure, mass
splitting, strict same-point scale cascades, separated active physical points,
and localized gauge degeneracy.

\begin{lemma}[Compactness failure]\label{lem:compactness-failure}
If the compact-cylinder suitable package is unbounded on a fixed rescaled
cylinder, then after passing to a subsequence the local \(C+D\) quantity is
unbounded on a comparable cylinder.  Hence the case contains a new local CKN
concentration core.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The local Caccioppoli estimate for suitable weak solutions gives the standard
smaller-cylinder control of \(A+E\) in terms of \(C+D\) on a slightly larger
cylinder.  Therefore, if \(C+D\) were bounded on all comparable larger
cylinders, then \(A+E+C+D\) would be bounded on the smaller cylinder.  The
contrapositive proves that failure of the suitable package forces failure of
the local \(C+D\) bound at a comparable scale, which is precisely another
local CKN concentration core.
\end{proof}

\section{Decoupling theorems}

\begin{assumption}[Local decoupling and scale rigidity]\label{ass:local-dec}
The following local decoupling theorems hold for active local Type II rescaled frames:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item comparable same-point cores combine into one compound core or make the
      localized gauge matrix degenerate;
\item strict same-point cascades reduce in the innermost active rescaled frame to a
      single-core or scale-rigidity case;
\item separated active physical points are locally invisible in each other's
      active rescaled frames after velocity, pressure, and modulation decoupling;
\item every scale-rigidity case produced by this reduction is excluded by the
      local Type I tangent criterion or by the local scale-rigidity theorem.
\end{enumerate}
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Local multibubble and cascade exclusion]\label{thm:multi}
Assume the local Type I tangent criterion, the local single-core Type II criterion,
and \cref{ass:local-dec}.  Then no local multibubble or cascade Type II case
can occur.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{lem:compactness-failure}, compactness failure produces another local
concentration core.  Passing to subsequences, active cores are comparable
at the same point, strictly
scale-separated at the same point, or separated in physical space.

Comparable cores form a compound core.  If this core is nondegenerate, the
single-core compactness criterion applies; otherwise the case is gauge-degenerate and is
covered by \cref{ass:local-dec}.  Strict same-point cascades and separated
active points are also reduced by \cref{ass:local-dec} to either the
single-core compactness criterion or a scale-rigidity case.  Both are excluded by the stated
hypotheses.  Therefore no multibubble or cascade case remains.
\end{proof}

\section{Consequence}

Together with the local CKN concentration result and the local
single-core compactness criterion, \cref{thm:multi} removes the entire Type II case after
the local Type I tangent criterion has been proved.
