\title[Scale Collapse for Type II Rescalings]{Scale-Collapse Dichotomy for Type II Navier--Stokes Rescalings}
\author{Guillem Cordero}
\date{}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
We analyze Type II Navier--Stokes rescalings for which the renormalized scale
collapses along the tail.  In a scale-translation gauge, the logarithmic scale
satisfies \(\partial_\tau\log\lambda=a\).  If the collapsing rescaling retains a
nonzero localized \(L^2\) core, then the negative part of the scale drift has
infinite localized accumulation.  This gives a scale-drift dichotomy.
If instead one seeks a structural PDE description of the same class,
then additional compactness, convergence of modulation parameters, and limiting profile hypotheses reduce
the rescaling to a nonzero stationary solution of a generalized self-similar
Navier--Stokes profile equation.  In parameter ranges covered by an appropriate
Liouville or rigidity theorem, the scale-collapse rescaling is excluded; outside
those ranges the remaining question is the corresponding stationary or
generalized self-similar Liouville problem.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Let \((u,p)\) be a suitable weak solution of the three-dimensional incompressible
Navier--Stokes equations
\[
  \partial_tu+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p=\nu\Delta u,
  \qquad \nabla\cdot u=0,
\]
near a putative Type II singular time.  We study a terminal rescaling written
in a scale-translation gauge:
\[
  u(x,t)=\lambda(\tau)^{-1}V\left(\frac{x-X(\tau)}{\lambda(\tau)},\tau\right),
  \qquad
  p(x,t)=\lambda(\tau)^{-2}P\left(\frac{x-X(\tau)}{\lambda(\tau)},\tau\right)+c(t),
\]
where \(\lambda(\tau)>0\).  The corresponding renormalized equation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:renorm-ns}
  \partial_\tau V+(V\cdot\nabla)V+\nabla P
  =\nu\Delta V+a(\tau)(V+y\cdot\nabla V)+b(\tau)\cdot\nabla V,
  \qquad \nabla\cdot V=0.
\end{equation}
The convention used throughout is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:log-scale-convention}
  \frac{d}{d\tau}\log\lambda(\tau)=a(\tau).
\end{equation}
Thus physical scale collapse \(\lambda(\tau)\to0\) is associated with negative
logarithmic scale drift.

The preceding reductions isolate compact, radiative, multibubble, and
rough-core mechanisms.  This paper treats the remaining scale-collapse mechanism
in two complementary ways.  First, it records the exact finite-or-infinite
alternative for the localized negative scale drift.  Second, it gives the
conditional autonomous-limit compactness-based reduction on thick scale-collapse windows.

The basic localized mass is
\[
  M_R(\tau)=\int_{\mathbb R^3}|V(y,\tau)|^2\Phi(\tau,y)\,dy,
\]
where \(0\le\Phi\le1\) is a smooth moving cutoff adapted to the scale-collapse
core.  The scale-drift density is
\[
  \mathcal C_{\rm sc}(\tau)=a_-(\tau)M_R(\tau),
  \qquad a_-(\tau)=\max\{-a(\tau),0\}.
\]
If \(\lambda(\tau)\to0\) and the localized core has a positive \(L^2\) floor,
then
\[
  \int_{\tau_0}^\infty \mathcal C_{\rm sc}(\tau)\,d\tau=\infty.
\]
This gives the localized scale-drift dichotomy.

The same scale-collapse class can also be analyzed through compactness.  On selected
windows \([\tau_n,\tau_n+T]\), if the translated solutions are locally compact
and if
\[
  a(\tau_n+\cdot)\to a_\infty,
  \qquad b(\tau_n+\cdot)\to b_\infty
\]
in a topology strong enough to pass the coefficient terms in distributions,
then a subsequence converges to a nonzero solution of the autonomous equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:autonomous-limit}
  \partial_s U+(U\cdot\nabla)U+\nabla\Pi
  =\nu\Delta U+a_\infty(U+y\cdot\nabla U)+b_\infty\cdot\nabla U,
  \qquad \nabla\cdot U=0.
\end{equation}
With an additional stationary limiting profile hypothesis, this yields a nonzero
stationary profile \((W,Q)\) satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stationary-profile}
  (W\cdot\nabla)W+\nabla Q
  =\nu\Delta W+a_\infty(W+y\cdot\nabla W)+b_\infty\cdot\nabla W,
  \qquad \nabla\cdot W=0.
\end{equation}
The exclusion is then supplied by the relevant Liouville or rigidity theorem
for \eqref{eq:stationary-profile}.  In the classical backward self-similar
normalization this is the role of the theorem of
Necas--Ruzicka--Sverak \cite{NecasRuzickaSverak1996}.  Other parameter ranges
must be stated as separate Liouville hypotheses; forward self-similar
Navier--Stokes profiles, for example, are not excluded by a blanket
nonexistence statement.

\begin{theorem}[Main scale-collapse alternative]\label{thm:intro-main}
Let \((V,P,a,b,\lambda)\) satisfy \eqref{eq:renorm-ns} and
\eqref{eq:log-scale-convention} on \([\tau_0,\infty)\).  Assume
\(\lambda(\tau)\to0\) and assume that the localized core has a positive
\(L^2\) floor.  Then the localized negative scale-drift cost has infinite total
accumulation:
\[
  \int_{\tau_0}^\infty a_-(\tau)M_R(\tau)\,d\tau=\infty.
\]
If, in addition, there are thick scale-collapse windows with local compactness and
convergent modulation coefficients, and a stationary limiting profile, then the
rescaling produces a nonzero stationary solution of \eqref{eq:stationary-profile}.
Consequently the scale-collapse rescaling is excluded in every parameter regime
where the corresponding stationary or generalized self-similar Liouville theorem
rules out such nonzero profiles in the admissible class.
\end{theorem}

\section{Scale drift and localized mass}

\begin{definition}[Localized core mass]
Let \(\Phi(\tau,\cdot)\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^3)\) be a nonnegative moving
cutoff.  The associated localized core mass is
\[
  M_R(\tau)=\int_{\mathbb R^3}|V(y,\tau)|^2\Phi(\tau,y)\,dy.
\]
We say that the rescaling has a nonvanishing localized core if there are
\(m_0>0\) and \(\tau_1\ge\tau_0\) such that
\[
  M_R(\tau)\ge m_0
  \qquad\text{for a.e. }\tau\ge\tau_1.
\]
We say that the localized core is bounded above on a family of windows if
\(M_R(\tau)\le M_1\) a.e. on those windows for some \(M_1<\infty\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Localized scale-drift cost]
The localized scale-drift density and its cumulative cost are
\[
  \mathcal C_{\rm sc}(\tau)=a_-(\tau)M_R(\tau),
  \qquad
  \mathcal C_{\rm sc}[\tau_0,\infty)
  =\int_{\tau_0}^\infty \mathcal C_{\rm sc}(\tau)\,d\tau.
\]
The density is nonnegative and measures the inward logarithmic scale drift
carried by the localized \(L^2\) core.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Finite-or-infinite alternative]\label{lem:finite-infinite}
For every measurable renormalized solution for which \(a_-M_R\) is measurable and
nonnegative, exactly one of the following alternatives holds:
\[
  \int_{\tau_0}^\infty a_-(\tau)M_R(\tau)\,d\tau<\infty,
  \qquad\text{or}\qquad
  \int_{\tau_0}^\infty a_-(\tau)M_R(\tau)\,d\tau=\infty.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The integral is an extended nonnegative number.  Hence it is either finite or
infinite, and the two cases are mutually exclusive.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Finite negative drift with bounded core mass]\label{lem:finite-negative-drift}
Assume \(M_R(\tau)\le M_*\) for a.e. \(\tau\ge\tau_0\) and
\[
  \int_{\tau_0}^\infty a_-(\tau)\,d\tau<\infty.
\]
Then
\[
  \int_{\tau_0}^\infty a_-(\tau)M_R(\tau)\,d\tau<\infty.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since \(a_-\ge0\),
\[
  \int_{\tau_0}^\infty a_-(\tau)M_R(\tau)\,d\tau
  \le M_*\int_{\tau_0}^\infty a_-(\tau)\,d\tau<\infty.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Logarithmic scale identity]\label{lem:log-scale}
Under the convention \eqref{eq:log-scale-convention}, for every
\(\tau_0\le\tau_1<\tau_2<\infty\),
\[
  \int_{\tau_1}^{\tau_2}a(\tau)\,d\tau
  =\log\lambda(\tau_2)-\log\lambda(\tau_1).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
This is the fundamental theorem of calculus applied to
\(\partial_\tau\log\lambda=a\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Collapse with a nonvanishing core forces infinite scale-drift cost]\label{thm:collapse-core-floor}
Assume
\[
  \lambda(\tau)\to0\qquad\text{as }\tau\to\infty,
\]
and assume that the rescaling has a nonvanishing localized core.  Then
\[
  \int_{\tau_0}^\infty a_-(\tau)M_R(\tau)\,d\tau=\infty.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{lem:log-scale},
\[
  \int_{\tau_1}^{\tau_2}a(\tau)\,d\tau
  =\log\lambda(\tau_2)-\log\lambda(\tau_1).
\]
Since \(\lambda(\tau)\to0\), the right-hand side tends to \(-\infty\) as
\(\tau_2\to\infty\).  If \(\int_{\tau_1}^\infty a_-(\tau)\,d\tau<\infty\), then
\[
  \int_{\tau_1}^{\tau_2}a(\tau)\,d\tau
  =\int_{\tau_1}^{\tau_2}a_+(\tau)\,d\tau
  -\int_{\tau_1}^{\tau_2}a_-(\tau)\,d\tau
  \ge -\int_{\tau_1}^\infty a_-(\tau)\,d\tau> -\infty,
\]
contradicting the divergence to \(-\infty\).  Thus
\(\int_{\tau_1}^\infty a_-(\tau)\,d\tau=\infty\).

The core floor gives \(M_R(\tau)\ge m_0\) for a.e. \(\tau\ge\tau_1\).  Hence
\[
  \int_{\tau_0}^\infty a_-(\tau)M_R(\tau)\,d\tau
  \ge m_0\int_{\tau_1}^\infty a_-(\tau)\,d\tau=\infty.
\]
\end{proof}

\section{Finite-cost classes and scale-collapse costs}

The previous theorem gives an infinite nonnegative quantity.  To use this
quantity as an exclusion mechanism, the finite-cost class under consideration
must be controlled by that quantity.  We state this as an analytic hypothesis on
the class of scale-collapse rescalings.

\begin{assumption}[Finite-cost class controlled by scale drift]\label{ass:scale-cost}
For the scale-collapse class under consideration, membership in the finite-cost
class implies finiteness of the nonnegative measurable functional
\[
  \mathcal C_{\rm sc}[\tau_0,\infty)
  =\int_{\tau_0}^\infty a_-(\tau)M_R(\tau)\,d\tau
\]
on the rescaling.  Equivalently, a rescaling for which
\(\mathcal C_{\rm sc}[\tau_0,\infty)=\infty\) does not belong to the
finite-cost class.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Cost exclusion for scale collapse]\label{thm:cost-exclusion}
Assume that the finite-cost class is controlled by scale drift.  If
\[
  \int_{\tau_0}^\infty a_-(\tau)M_R(\tau)\,d\tau=\infty,
\]
then the finite-cost scale-collapse rescaling is excluded.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The divergent localized scale-drift cost is incompatible with membership in the
finite-cost class by \cref{ass:scale-cost}.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Absolute scale cost]
Let
\[
  \mathcal C_{\rm abs}(\tau)
  =\nu\int_{\mathbb R^3}|\nabla V(y,\tau)|^2\Phi(\tau,y)\,dy
  +|a(\tau)|M_R(\tau).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Scale-drift divergence implies absolute cost divergence]\label{lem:absolute-cost}
If
\[
  \int_{\tau_0}^\infty a_-(\tau)M_R(\tau)\,d\tau=\infty,
\]
then
\[
  \int_{\tau_0}^\infty \mathcal C_{\rm abs}(\tau)\,d\tau=\infty.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Pointwise,
\[
  \mathcal C_{\rm abs}(\tau)
  \ge |a(\tau)|M_R(\tau)
  \ge a_-(\tau)M_R(\tau).
\]
Integrating gives the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Absolute cost exclusion]\label{cor:absolute-cost-exclusion}
If the finite-cost class is controlled by \(\int\mathcal C_{\rm abs}\), then
every rescaling satisfying the conclusion of \cref{thm:collapse-core-floor} is
excluded from the finite-cost class.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply \cref{lem:absolute-cost} and then the assumed absolute cost criterion.
\end{proof}

\section{Thick drift and autonomous reduction}

The infinite weighted drift in \cref{thm:collapse-core-floor} does not by itself
produce a fixed-length window with a uniform negative average.  A bounded
nonnegative function may have infinite integral while its unit-window averages
tend to zero.  The autonomous profile reduction therefore requires a
separate thickness hypothesis.

\begin{definition}[Thick negative-drift windows]
The rescaling has thick negative-drift windows if there are \(T>0\), \(c>0\), and
\(\tau_n\to\infty\) such that
\[
  \frac1T\int_{\tau_n}^{\tau_n+T}a_-(\tau)M_R(\tau)\,d\tau\ge c
  \qquad\text{for every }n.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Autonomous parameter limit]
On the same windows \([\tau_n,\tau_n+T]\), assume there are constants
\(a_\infty\in\mathbb R\) and \(b_\infty\in\mathbb R^3\) such that
\[
  a(\tau_n+\cdot)\to a_\infty,
  \qquad
  b(\tau_n+\cdot)\to b_\infty
\]
in a coefficient topology sufficient to pass the products
\(a(V+y\cdot\nabla V)\) and \(b\cdot\nabla V\) to the limit in distributions.
Strong convergence in \(L^1(0,T)\) is sufficient.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Thick drift gives a negative autonomous scale parameter]\label{lem:negative-parameter}
Assume thick negative-drift windows, assume \(M_R\le M_1\) on these windows, and
assume \(a(\tau_n+\cdot)\to a_\infty\) in \(L^1(0,T)\).  Then
\[
  a_\infty\le -c/M_1<0.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The upper mass bound and thick weighted drift give
\[
  \frac1T\int_{\tau_n}^{\tau_n+T}a_-(\tau)\,d\tau\ge c/M_1.
\]
After translation and \(L^1(0,T)\) convergence of \(a\) to the constant
\(a_\infty\), the left-hand side converges to \((-a_\infty)_+\).  Therefore
\((-a_\infty)_+\ge c/M_1\), which gives the claim.
\end{proof}

\section{Autonomous limit reduction}

\begin{assumption}[Compactness on selected windows]\label{ass:compactness}
For the selected windows \([\tau_n,\tau_n+T]\), define
\[
  V_n(y,s)=V(y,\tau_n+s),
  \qquad P_n(y,s)=P(y,\tau_n+s),
  \qquad 0<s<T.
\]
For every bounded ball \(B_R\), the sequence \((V_n)\) is precompact strongly in
\(L^2(0,T;L^q(B_R))\) for every \(q<6\), weakly compact in
\(L^2(0,T;H^1(B_R))\), and weak-* compact in
\(L^\infty(0,T;L^3(B_R))\).  The pressures are normalized modulo
functions of time and converge locally in a topology sufficient to pass
\[
  -\Delta P=\partial_i\partial_j(V_iV_j)
\]
to the limit.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Autonomous reduced limit]\label{thm:autonomous-limit}
Assume compactness on selected windows, assume uniform local CKN bounds on each
compact cylinder, assume the autonomous parameter limit, and assume that a
localized \(L^2\) floor passes to the limit on a fixed compact interior region.  Then,
after passing to a subsequence, \((V_n,P_n)\) converges to a nonzero weak
solution \((U,\Pi)\) of \eqref{eq:autonomous-limit} on
\(\mathbb R^3\times(0,T)\).  Moreover
\[
  U\in L^\infty(0,T;L^3_{\rm loc}(\mathbb R^3)),
\]
and the pressure satisfies
\[
  -\Delta\Pi=\partial_i\partial_j(U_iU_j)
\]
modulo functions of \(s\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The local weak-* \(L^\infty L^3\) bounds follow from the uniform local CKN bounds on compact cylinders.
The local \(L^2H^1\) bound and time compactness are included in
\cref{ass:compactness}, so Aubin--Lions compactness gives strong local
convergence in \(L^2(0,T;L^q(B_R))\) for every \(q<6\).  This strong local
convergence, together with weak \(L^2H^1\) convergence, passes the nonlinear
term to \((U\cdot\nabla)U\) in distributions.  The pressure convergence passes
the Poisson reconstruction to \(\Pi\).

The autonomous parameter limit passes the coefficient terms to
\(a_\infty(U+y\cdot\nabla U)\) and \(b_\infty\cdot\nabla U\).  Hence the limit
solves \eqref{eq:autonomous-limit}.  The localized \(L^2\) floor passes
to the limit by the assumed convergence on the compact interior region, so
\(U\not\equiv0\).
\end{proof}

\section{Stationary profiles and rigidity}

The autonomous limit theorem gives a solution on translated finite windows.  It
does not by itself produce a stationary profile.  That step requires an
additional asymptotic compactness or Lyapunov input.

\begin{assumption}[Stationary limiting profile]\label{ass:stationary-omega}
The autonomous limit \((U,\Pi)\) produced by \cref{thm:autonomous-limit} has a
nonzero stationary limiting profile \((W,Q)\) satisfying
\[
  W\in L^3_{\rm loc}(\mathbb R^3),
\]
and
\[
  (W\cdot\nabla)W+\nabla Q
  =\nu\Delta W+a_\infty(W+y\cdot\nabla W)+b_\infty\cdot\nabla W,
  \qquad \nabla\cdot W=0.
\]
The solution belongs to the admissibility class required by the rigidity theorem
used for the parameter pair \((a_\infty,b_\infty)\); if that theorem requires
spatial decay or integrability, those requirements are part of the rigidity
hypothesis, not of the Type II reduction.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Stationary rigidity for the limiting parameters]\label{ass:rigidity}
For the parameter pair \((a_\infty,b_\infty)\), every admissible stationary
solution \((W,Q)\) of \eqref{eq:stationary-profile} in the class specified by
the relevant Liouville or rigidity theorem is either zero or cannot arise as a
terminal Type II scale-collapse profile in the class under consideration.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional self-similar exclusion]\label{thm:selfsimilar-exclusion}
Assume the hypotheses of \cref{thm:autonomous-limit}, the stationary limiting profile
assumption, and the stationary rigidity assumption for \((a_\infty,b_\infty)\).
Then the scale-collapse rescaling is excluded in that parameter regime.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{thm:autonomous-limit}, the rescaling produces a nonzero autonomous limit.
By the stationary limiting profile assumption, this yields a nonzero stationary
profile \((W,Q)\) in the admissible class for \((a_\infty,b_\infty)\).  The
stationary rigidity assumption says that no such nonzero profile can arise from
a terminal Type II scale-collapse profile in the class under consideration.  This
contradicts the assumption that the rescaling belongs to that class.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Classical and nonclassical parameter regimes]
When \(a_\infty<0\) and the parameters can be reduced to the classical backward
self-similar normalization, the needed rigidity is modeled on the
Necas--Ruzicka--Sverak theorem excluding nontrivial Leray self-similar profiles
in the classical admissible class \cite{NecasRuzickaSverak1996}.  For other
negative values, for drifted equations with \(b_\infty\ne0\), or for the
stationary case \(a_\infty=0\), the paper records the corresponding Liouville
question as an explicit assumption unless an appropriate theorem is supplied.
Forward self-similar solution classes require special care because nontrivial
forward self-similar Navier--Stokes solutions may exist in large-data settings.
\end{remark}

\section{Summary of cases}

The scale-collapse class is therefore divided as follows.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item If the localized negative scale-drift cost is finite, then the moving
localized energy procedure may absorb the negative scale term together with the
remaining finite error terms.
\item If the localized negative scale-drift cost is infinite and the finite-cost
class is controlled by that cost, then the finite-cost scale-collapse rescaling is
excluded.
\item If one does not use the cost criterion but has thick windows, compactness,
modulation convergence, a stationary limiting profile, and parameter-specific
rigidity, then the rescaling is excluded through the generalized self-similar
profile equation.
\item If the infinite drift is thin or oscillatory, or if compactness,
modulation convergence, the stationary limiting profile, or the relevant Liouville
theorem is unavailable, the remaining obstruction is the corresponding PDE
hypothesis listed above.
\end{enumerate}
