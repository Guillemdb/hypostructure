# S9: scattering branch discharge

This note proves that small critical \(L^3\) profiles are perturbative
Navier-Stokes profiles and therefore cannot be active Type II bubbles. The
output certificate is
\[
K_{\mathrm{ScattBranch}}^-,
\]
meaning that after the scattering ledger is removed, every remaining active
profile has a fixed positive critical mass floor.

---

## S9.1 (Critical mild solution space)

For a time interval \(I=[0,T]\) or \(I=[0,\infty)\), define the Kato
critical space \(X(I)\) by the norm
\[
\|u\|_{X(I)}
:=
\sup_{t\in I}\|u(t)\|_{L^3_x}
+
\sup_{\substack{t\in I\\ t>0}}
t^{1/8}\|u(t)\|_{L^4_x}.
\]
Both terms are invariant under the Navier-Stokes scaling.
Let \(\mathbb P\) be the Leray projector and define
\[
B(u,v)(t)
:=
-\int_0^t e^{\nu(t-s)\Delta}
\mathbb P\nabla\cdot(u\otimes v)(s)\,ds .
\]

The critical mild formulation of NS3D is
\[
u(t)=e^{\nu t\Delta}u_0+B(u,u)(t).
\]

### Lemma S9.1 (critical heat and bilinear estimates)

There are constants \(A_\nu,B_\nu<\infty\) such that for all
divergence-free \(u_0\in L^3(\mathbb R^3)\),
\[
\|e^{\nu t\Delta}u_0\|_{X([0,\infty))}
\le
A_\nu\|u_0\|_{L^3},
\]
and for all \(u,v\in X([0,\infty))\),
\[
\|B(u,v)\|_{X([0,\infty))}
\le
B_\nu\|u\|_{X([0,\infty))}\|v\|_{X([0,\infty))}.
\]

#### Proof

The \(L^\infty_tL^3_x\) heat estimate follows from contractivity of the heat
semigroup on \(L^3\):
\[
\|e^{\nu t\Delta}u_0\|_3\le\|u_0\|_3.
\]
The \(L^4\) Kato estimate follows from heat smoothing:
\[
\|e^{\nu t\Delta}u_0\|_4
\le
C_\nu t^{-1/8}\|u_0\|_3.
\]
This is the case \(p=3,q=4\) of the heat kernel bound
\[
\|e^{\nu t\Delta}f\|_q
\le
C_{\nu,p,q}t^{-\frac32(\frac1p-\frac1q)}\|f\|_p
\].
Multiplying by \(t^{1/8}\) and taking the supremum gives the second part of
the \(X\)-bound.

For the bilinear term, write
\[
e^{\nu(t-s)\Delta}\mathbb P\nabla\cdot F
\]
as convolution with a kernel satisfying
\[
\|e^{\nu(t-s)\Delta}\mathbb P\nabla\cdot F\|_q
\le
C_{\nu,p,q}
(t-s)^{-\frac12-\frac32(\frac1p-\frac1q)}
\|F\|_p.
\]
Apply this with \(F=u\otimes v\), Hölder
\[
\|u(s)\otimes v(s)\|_2
\le
\|u(s)\|_4\|v(s)\|_4
\le
s^{-1/4}\|u\|_X\|v\|_X,
\]
and use the kernel estimates with \(p=2,q=3\) and \(p=2,q=4\). For the
\(L^3\)-part,
\[
\|B(u,v)(t)\|_3
\le
C\|u\|_X\|v\|_X
\int_0^t(t-s)^{-3/4}s^{-1/4}\,ds
\le
C\|u\|_X\|v\|_X.
\]
For the \(L^4\)-part,
\[
\|B(u,v)(t)\|_4
\le
C\|u\|_X\|v\|_X
\int_0^t(t-s)^{-7/8}s^{-1/4}\,ds
\le
Ct^{-1/8}\|u\|_X\|v\|_X.
\]
Multiplying the last estimate by \(t^{1/8}\), taking the supremum in \(t\),
and combining both components gives
\[
\|B(u,v)\|_{X}\le B_\nu\|u\|_X\|v\|_X.
\]

\(\square\)

## S9.2 (Small \(L^3\) profiles are perturbative)

### Theorem S9.2 (small-data critical NS)

There exists
\[
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{sd}}>0
\]
such that if \(u_0\in L^3(\mathbb R^3)\) is divergence-free and
\[
\|u_0\|_3\le\varepsilon_{\mathrm{sd}},
\]
then NS3D has a unique global mild solution \(u\in X([0,\infty))\) satisfying
\[
\|u\|_{X([0,\infty))}
\le
2A_\nu\|u_0\|_3.
\]
Moreover \(u\) is perturbative in the sense that, for every finite interval
\([0,T]\),
\[
\|u-e^{\nu t\Delta}u_0\|_{X([0,T])}
\le
C_\nu\|u_0\|_3^2.
\]

#### Proof

Choose
\[
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{sd}}
\le
\frac{1}{4A_\nu B_\nu}.
\]
Let
\[
R:=2A_\nu\|u_0\|_3
\]
and consider the closed ball
\[
\mathcal B_R:=\{u\in X([0,\infty)):\|u\|_X\le R\}.
\]
Define
\[
\Phi(u):=e^{\nu t\Delta}u_0+B(u,u).
\]
For \(u\in\mathcal B_R\), Lemma S9.1 gives
\[
\|\Phi(u)\|_X
\le
A_\nu\|u_0\|_3+B_\nu R^2.
\]
Since \(R=2A_\nu\|u_0\|_3\),
\[
B_\nu R^2
=
4B_\nu A_\nu^2\|u_0\|_3^2
\le
A_\nu\|u_0\|_3
\]
by the definition of \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{sd}}\). Hence
\[
\|\Phi(u)\|_X\le R.
\]
For \(u,v\in\mathcal B_R\),
\[
\Phi(u)-\Phi(v)
=
B(u-v,u)+B(v,u-v),
\]
so
\[
\|\Phi(u)-\Phi(v)\|_X
\le
B_\nu(\|u\|_X+\|v\|_X)\|u-v\|_X
\le
2B_\nu R\|u-v\|_X.
\]
The smallness condition gives
\[
2B_\nu R
=
4A_\nu B_\nu\|u_0\|_3
\le
\frac12.
\]
Thus \(\Phi\) is a contraction on \(\mathcal B_R\), and Banach's fixed point
theorem gives a unique global mild solution with the asserted bound.

The perturbative estimate follows from
\[
u-e^{\nu t\Delta}u_0=B(u,u)
\]
and Lemma S9.1:
\[
\|u-e^{\nu t\Delta}u_0\|_X
\le
B_\nu\|u\|_X^2
\le
4B_\nu A_\nu^2\|u_0\|_3^2.
\]

\(\square\)

## S9.3 (Small profiles are not active Type II profiles)

Define
\[
K_{\mathrm{ScattProfile}}^+
\]
for a profile \(\phi\) to mean that the nonlinear NS3D profile generated by
\(\phi\) is global in the critical mild class \(X([0,\infty))\), obeys the
small-data estimate of Theorem S9.2, and belongs to the perturbative
remainder ledger on every compact renormalized window.

### Corollary S9.3

If \(\phi\in L^3(\mathbb R^3)\) is divergence-free and
\[
\|\phi\|_3\le\varepsilon_{\mathrm{sd}},
\]
then
\[
K_{\mathrm{ScattProfile}}^+
\]
holds for \(\phi\). In particular, \(\phi\) cannot be an active Type II
profile.

#### Proof

Theorem S9.2 gives the global critical mild solution \(u^\phi\) with
\[
\|u^\phi\|_{X([0,\infty))}
\le
2A_\nu\|\phi\|_3.
\]
The \(X\)-norm is invariant under the Navier-Stokes scaling
\[
u(x,t)\mapsto
\lambda u(\lambda x,\lambda^2 t).
\]
Therefore every rescaled copy of \(u^\phi\) remains uniformly small in the
same critical topology on compact windows. The perturbative estimate shows
that its nonlinear contribution is controlled by the heat evolution plus a
quadratic \(O(\|\phi\|_3^2)\) error. Such a profile is assigned to the
scattering remainder ledger by definition of \(K_{\mathrm{ScattProfile}}^+\)
and cannot be an active singular profile.

\(\square\)

## S9.4 (Scattering branch discharge)

### Theorem S9.4

For every critical NS3D profile package satisfying the small-data stability
ledger, the active profile set satisfies the mass floor
\[
\phi^j\ \text{active}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\|\phi^j\|_3>\varepsilon_{\mathrm{sd}}.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
K_{\mathrm{ScattBranch}}^-
\]
holds: after removing the scattering ledger, no small-data profile remains in
the active Type II branch.

#### Proof

Let \(\phi^j\) be a profile with
\[
\|\phi^j\|_3\le\varepsilon_{\mathrm{sd}}.
\]
By Corollary S9.3, \(\phi^j\) emits \(K_{\mathrm{ScattProfile}}^+\), so its
nonlinear evolution is global and perturbative in the critical stability
space. Hence it is placed in the scattering remainder ledger. By definition,
active Type II profiles are precisely those not removed by the perturbative
scattering ledger. Therefore \(\phi^j\) is not active. Taking the
contrapositive gives the displayed mass floor for every active profile.

\(\square\)
