\title[Ancient Navier--Stokes solutions under axisymmetry and decay]{Ancient Navier--Stokes solutions under axisymmetry and decay}
\author{Guillem Duran Ballester}
\date{}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
We consider ancient solutions of the three-dimensional Navier--Stokes equations
arising in the Type I blow-up analysis under three additional structures:
axisymmetry without swirl, axisymmetry with a quantitative bound on the angular
velocity, and weighted spatial decay in self-similar variables.  In the no-swirl
case, the Liouville theorem of Koch--Nadirashvili--Seregin--Sverak, applied
after a time shift, implies that every smooth Type I ancient solution is
identically zero.  We also use two swirl-bearing axisymmetric Liouville
theorems: the Lei--Zhang--Zhao theorem for
\(\Gamma=rU_\theta\in L_t^\infty L_x^p\), \(1\le p<\infty\), and the
Lei--Ren--Zhang theorem for \(z\)-periodic ancient solutions with bounded
\(\Gamma\).  The pointwise controlled-swirl and weighted alternatives are
reduced to explicitly stated fixed-exponent Liouville hypotheses.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Let \(U:\mathbb R^{3}\times(-\infty,0)\to\mathbb R^{3}\) and
\(P:\mathbb R^{3}\times(-\infty,0)\to\mathbb R\) solve
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns}
\partial_t U-\Delta U+U\cdot\nabla U+\nabla P=0,\qquad
\nabla\cdot U=0.
\end{equation}
The Type I analysis of a finite-time singularity leads, after rescaling about a
singular point, to nonzero mild ancient solutions satisfying the Type I bound
\cite{Paper0LocalConcentration,Paper1TypeI,Seregin2012,SereginSverak2009}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:type-i}
\sup_{t<0}\sqrt{-t}\,\|U(\cdot,t)\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R^3)}<\infty .
\end{equation}
Throughout the paper, a nonzero ancient limit means an ancient solution
obtained from the blow-up procedure with the normalization fixed in the
companion papers: the limiting solution carries a positive
pressure-normalized local critical density, as in Seregin's compactness
argument
\cite{Paper0LocalConcentration,Paper1TypeI,Seregin2012}.  The present paper
records three additional structures under which such limits can be excluded.

The first case is axisymmetry without swirl.  In this case the argument is
settled by the Liouville theorem asserting that bounded ancient axisymmetric
no-swirl solutions are constant.  Since a solution satisfying \eqref{eq:type-i}
is bounded on every time interval \((-\infty,T]\), \(T<0\), this theorem
applies after a time shift.  The Type I decay as \(t\to-\infty\) then
eliminates the constant.

The second case is axisymmetry with swirl satisfying a quantitative bound.
The available estimates for the angular component are substantially weaker than
in the no-swirl case.  Two cases, however, are covered by published Liouville
theorems: finite \(L^p\) control of the swirl variable
\(\Gamma=rU_\theta\), and axial periodicity with bounded \(\Gamma\).  After
recording these inputs, we formulate the remaining pointwise controlled-swirl
criterion as a fixed-exponent Liouville hypothesis, with \(\gamma>0\) fixed
throughout the statement.

The third case is uniform spatial decay in self-similar variables.  This
condition rules out spatial escape in weighted \(L^2\), but it does not, by
itself, furnish a time-dependent Liouville theorem.  We therefore isolate the
weighted Liouville hypothesis and prove the reduction from that hypothesis.

\subsection{Normalization convention}

Every exclusion below is applied to an ancient limit produced by a singularity.
Such a limit is a bounded ancient solution together with the nontriviality
normalization inherited from the blow-up sequence: it carries a fixed positive
pressure-normalized local critical density.  This is the nonzero ancient-limit
normalization obtained from the positive local concentration and compactness
persistence statements in
\cite[Theorem \texttt{thm:singular-positive} and Proposition
\texttt{prop:compactness-persistence}]{Paper0LocalConcentration} and from the
selected-scale nontriviality theorem
\cite[Proposition \texttt{prop:nonzero}]{Paper1TypeI}.  Therefore any
Liouville theorem which concludes \(U\equiv0\) contradicts the construction of
the limit.  Apart from this nontriviality normalization, the arguments below
use only the stated PDE properties of the ancient solution.

\begin{lemma}[Nonzero normalization of blow-up limits]
\label{lem:nonzero-normalization}
Let \(U\) be a Type I ancient limit obtained from the blow-up procedure used in
the companion papers.  Then \(U\not\equiv0\) on
\(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,0)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The blow-up construction fixes a positive pressure-normalized local critical
density for the limiting solution.  The persistence of this positive density
under the local compactness passage is
\cite[Proposition \texttt{prop:compactness-persistence}]
{Paper0LocalConcentration}, and the selected Type I ancient limit is proved
nonzero in \cite[Proposition \texttt{prop:nonzero}]{Paper1TypeI}.  The
identically zero velocity has vanishing local critical velocity density and,
under the pressure gauge convention used throughout this paper, vanishing
pressure-normalized density.  Hence the normalized ancient limit cannot be the
zero solution.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Notation and PDE conventions}

For a time interval \(I\subset\mathbb R\) and a measurable function \(f\), we
write
\[
   \|f\|_{L_t^\infty L_x^p(\mathbb R^3\times I)}
   =
   \operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{t\in I}
   \|f(\cdot,t)\|_{L^p(\mathbb R^3)},\qquad 1\le p<\infty ,
\]
and
\[
   \|f\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R^3\times I)}
   =
   \operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{(x,t)\in\mathbb R^3\times I}|f(x,t)|.
\]
All solutions considered in the proofs are smooth, so these essential suprema
agree with the corresponding pointwise suprema after redefining on null sets.
The subscript on the time norm records only the time variable being used; for
example \(L_s^\infty L_x^p(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,0))\) and
\(L_t^\infty L_x^p(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,0))\) denote the same mixed
norm after renaming the time coordinate.
The heat semigroup \(e^{t\Delta}\) and the Leray projector \(\mathbb P\) are
the whole-space operators on \(\mathbb R^3\); see, for example, Sohr's
functional-analytic treatment of the Navier--Stokes equations \cite{Sohr2001}.
The pressure is always understood modulo functions of time, since only
\(\nabla P\) appears in \eqref{eq:ns}; invariance of the equation and the
local energy inequality under such pressure gauges is recorded in
\cite[Lemma \texttt{lem:gauges-harmless}]{Paper1TypeI}.  The scale-invariant
pressure-normalized quantities used in the blow-up construction are those of
\cite[Proposition \texttt{prop:critical-scaling}]
{Paper0LocalConcentration}.
When an axisymmetric scalar such as
\(\Gamma_U(r,z,t)=rU_\theta(r,z,t)\) is placed in an \(L^p_x(\mathbb R^3)\)
space, it is regarded as the corresponding function of
\(x=(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta,z)\), independent of \(\theta\).  Thus, for
example,
\[
   \|\Gamma_U(\cdot,t)\|_{L^p(\mathbb R^3)}^p
   =
   2\pi\int_{\mathbb R}\int_0^\infty
      |\Gamma_U(r,z,t)|^p\,r\,dr\,dz,
   \qquad 1\le p<\infty .
\]
The axis \(\{r=0\}\) has three-dimensional Lebesgue measure zero, so the value
assigned to a cylindrical scalar on the axis does not affect its \(L^p\)-class;
when a pointwise on-axis value is needed, it is supplied by smooth extension of
the underlying Cartesian field.
An axisymmetric solution is called \(z\)-periodic if there exists a number
\(L>0\) such that its Cartesian velocity field satisfies
\[
   U(x_1,x_2,z+L,t)=U(x_1,x_2,z,t)
\]
for every \((x_1,x_2,z,t)\in\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,0)\).  Equivalently,
all cylindrical components are \(L\)-periodic in the axial variable \(z\) for
\(r>0\); the Cartesian formulation is the definition used at the axis.  No
periodicity of the pressure itself is assumed; only \(\nabla P\) enters the
Navier--Stokes equation, and the pressure is always understood up to a
time-dependent gauge.

\section{Ancient Type I solutions and self-similar variables}

\begin{definition}\label{def:type-i-ancient}
A smooth mild solution \(U\) of \eqref{eq:ns} on
\(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,0)\) is called a Type I ancient solution if
\eqref{eq:type-i} holds.  Here mild is understood in the standard velocity
formulation, equivalently by the Duhamel formula on each interval
\([a,b]\subset(-\infty,0)\) \cite{Kato1984,Sohr2001}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mild-formula}
   U(\cdot,t)=e^{(t-s)\Delta}U(\cdot,s)
   -\int_s^t e^{(t-\sigma)\Delta}\mathbb P\nabla\cdot
      (U(\cdot,\sigma)\otimes U(\cdot,\sigma))\,d\sigma,
      \qquad -\infty<s<t<0,
\end{equation}
in the sense of distributions, where \(\mathbb P\) denotes the Leray
projection.  Since all solutions considered below are smooth and bounded on
compact subintervals of \((-\infty,0)\), the tensor
\(U(\cdot,\sigma)\otimes U(\cdot,\sigma)\) is locally bounded in space-time;
the Duhamel integral is therefore a well-defined distributional integral on
each finite time interval.  Taking the divergence of \eqref{eq:ns} gives the
standard pressure identity
\[
   -\Delta P=\partial_i\partial_j(U_iU_j).
\]
This pressure reconstruction and the associated time-dependent gauge ambiguity
are already established for the series in
\cite[Lemmas \texttt{lem:gauges-harmless} and \texttt{lem:pressure}]
{Paper1TypeI} and
\cite[Theorem \texttt{thm:gauges} and Lemma \texttt{lem:mild-to-projected}]
{Paper2TightAncient}; we use that convention here without reproving it.
The velocity and pressure in backward self-similar variables are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:self-similar}
V(y,\tau)=\sqrt{-t}\,U(x,t),\qquad
Q(y,\tau)=(-t)P(x,t),\qquad
y=\frac{x}{\sqrt{-t}},\qquad
\tau=-\log(-t).
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Self-similar equation]\label{lem:self-similar-equation}
Let \((U,P)\) be a smooth solution of \eqref{eq:ns}, and define \((V,Q)\) by
\eqref{eq:self-similar}.  Then \(V\) is defined on
\(\mathbb R^3\times\mathbb R\) and satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:leray}
\partial_\tau V-\Delta V+\frac12 y\cdot\nabla V+\frac12 V
 +V\cdot\nabla V+\nabla Q=0,\qquad \nabla\cdot V=0.
\end{equation}
Conversely, every smooth solution \((V,Q)\) of \eqref{eq:leray} determines a
smooth solution of \eqref{eq:ns} by the inverse change of variables.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The asserted equation is the backward self-similar formulation used throughout
the Type I part of the series.  The chain-rule computation, including the
pressure scaling and the divergence equation, is given in
\cite[Lemma \texttt{lem:renormalized-equation}]{Paper1TypeI}.  That lemma
writes the equation as
\[
   \partial_\tau V+(V\cdot\nabla)V+\nabla Q
   =
   \Delta V-\frac12(V+y\cdot\nabla V),
\]
which is algebraically the same as \eqref{eq:leray} after moving all terms to
the left-hand side.  The inverse statement follows from the same
diffeomorphic change of variables, also recorded there.
\end{proof}

The Type I bound is equivalent to the uniform \(L^\infty\) bound for \(V\):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:type-i-v}
\sup_{\tau\in\mathbb R}\|V(\cdot,\tau)\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R^3)}<\infty .
\end{equation}
Indeed, if \(t=-e^{-\tau}\), then
\[
   (y,\tau)\longmapsto (x,t)=\bigl(e^{-\tau/2}y,-e^{-\tau}\bigr)
\]
is a smooth bijection from
\(\mathbb R^3\times\mathbb R\) onto
\(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,0)\), with inverse
\[
   y=\frac{x}{\sqrt{-t}},\qquad \tau=-\log(-t).
\]
For each fixed \(\tau\), the spatial map \(y\mapsto x=e^{-\tau/2}y\) is a
bijection of \(\mathbb R^3\).  Therefore
\[
\|V(\cdot,\tau)\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R^3)}
=\sqrt{-t}\,\|U(\cdot,t)\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R^3)}.
\]
Taking the supremum over \(\tau\in\mathbb R\) is therefore the same as taking
the supremum over \(t<0\), proving the equivalence of
\eqref{eq:type-i} and \eqref{eq:type-i-v}.

For axisymmetric flows we write
\[
x=(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta,z),\qquad
U=U_r e_r+U_\theta e_\theta+U_z e_z.
\]
These component functions are defined on the open set \(r>0\).  Since the
velocity is smooth on \(\mathbb R^3\), every identity involving cylindrical
components is first interpreted for \(r>0\) and then extended to the axis by
smoothness when an on-axis value is needed.  The flow is said to have no swirl
if \(U_\theta\equiv0\) for \(r>0\).  The same convention is used for \(V\),
with cylindrical coordinates
\[
y=(\rho\cos\theta,\rho\sin\theta,Z).
\]
We write
\[
   \Gamma_U(r,z,t)=rU_\theta(r,z,t),\qquad
   \Gamma_V(\rho,Z,\tau)=\rho V_\theta(\rho,Z,\tau).
\]
Under the self-similar change of variables these quantities agree:
\(\Gamma_V(\rho,Z,\tau)=\Gamma_U(r,z,t)\), where
\((r,z)=\sqrt{-t}\,(\rho,Z)\).

\begin{lemma}[Preservation of the structured hypotheses]
\label{lem:structure-preserved}
Axisymmetry and the no-swirl condition are preserved by the self-similar change
of variables \eqref{eq:self-similar}.  For any fixed \(\gamma>0\), the bounds
\begin{equation}\label{eq:swirl-control}
\sup_{\tau\in\mathbb R}\sup_{\rho>0,\ Z\in\mathbb R}
\rho^\gamma |V_\theta(\rho,Z,\tau)|<\infty
\end{equation}
are invariant under translations of \(\tau\).  If \(U\) is axisymmetric, then
the self-similar swirl variables satisfy
\[
   \Gamma_V(\rho,Z,\tau)=\Gamma_U(r,z,t),
   \qquad (r,z)=\sqrt{-t}\,(\rho,Z).
\]
Moreover, if \(U\) satisfies \eqref{eq:type-i}, then for every \(T<0\) the
restriction of \(U\) to \(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,T]\) is bounded.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The self-similar change of variables is a scalar dilation in space together with
a scalar multiplication of the velocity.  It therefore commutes with rotations
around the \(z\)-axis.  More explicitly, for every rotation \(R_\alpha\) around
the symmetry axis,
\[
   U(R_\alpha x,t)=R_\alpha U(x,t)
\]
implies
\[
   V(R_\alpha y,\tau)
   =\sqrt{-t}\,U(R_\alpha x,t)
   =R_\alpha\sqrt{-t}\,U(x,t)
   =R_\alpha V(y,\tau).
\]
Thus axisymmetry is preserved.  Since the cylindrical basis
\((e_r,e_\theta,e_z)\) is unchanged by a radial dilation, the component
relation is
\[
   V_\theta(\rho,Z,\tau)=\sqrt{-t}\,
      U_\theta(r,z,t),
   \qquad r=\sqrt{-t}\rho,\quad z=\sqrt{-t}Z .
\]
Consequently \(U_\theta\equiv0\) if and only if \(V_\theta\equiv0\), and
\[
   \Gamma_V(\rho,Z,\tau)
   =\rho V_\theta(\rho,Z,\tau)
   =\rho\sqrt{-t}\,U_\theta(r,z,t)
   =rU_\theta(r,z,t)
   =\Gamma_U(r,z,t).
\]
The expression \(\rho^\gamma |V_\theta|\) is written directly in
self-similar coordinates, so \eqref{eq:swirl-control} is unchanged when
\(V(y,\tau)\) is replaced by \(V(y,\tau+a)\), \(a\in\mathbb R\); the supremum is
over all \(\tau\in\mathbb R\).  Finally, if \(t\le T<0\), then
\eqref{eq:type-i} gives
\[
\|U(\cdot,t)\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R^3)}
\le
\frac{1}{\sqrt{-T}}
\sup_{s<0}\sqrt{-s}\,\|U(\cdot,s)\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R^3)} .
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Time shifts away from the singular time]\label{lem:time-shift}
Let \(U\) be a smooth Type I ancient solution of \eqref{eq:ns}.  Fix
\(T<0\), and set
\[
   W_T(x,s)=U(x,T+s),\qquad \Pi_T(x,s)=P(x,T+s),\qquad s<0.
\]
Then \((W_T,\Pi_T)\) is a smooth bounded mild ancient solution of
\eqref{eq:ns} on \(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,0)\).  If \(U\) is axisymmetric,
has no swirl, is periodic in \(z\), or satisfies an \(L_t^\infty L_x^p\) or
\(L^\infty\) bound on \(\Gamma_U=rU_\theta\), then \(W_T\) has the same
corresponding property on \(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,0)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Smoothness and the differential equations are invariant under translations in
time, since \(\partial_s W_T(x,s)=\partial_tU(x,T+s)\), all spatial
derivatives are unchanged, and
\[
   \nabla\Pi_T(x,s)=\nabla P(x,T+s).
\]
Consequently
\[
   \partial_s W_T-\Delta W_T+W_T\cdot\nabla W_T+\nabla\Pi_T
   =
   \bigl(\partial_t U-\Delta U+U\cdot\nabla U+\nabla P\bigr)(x,T+s)
   =0,
\]
and \(\nabla\cdot W_T=(\nabla\cdot U)(x,T+s)=0\).  The mild identity follows
by applying \eqref{eq:mild-formula} to \(U\) at the times \(T+s<T+t<0\).
Namely, for
\(-\infty<s<t<0\),
\[
   U(\cdot,T+t)
   =
   e^{(t-s)\Delta}U(\cdot,T+s)
   -\int_{T+s}^{T+t}
      e^{(T+t-\eta)\Delta}\mathbb P\nabla\cdot
      (U(\cdot,\eta)\otimes U(\cdot,\eta))\,d\eta .
\]
Set \(\eta=T+\sigma\).  Then \(d\eta=d\sigma\), the interval
\([T+s,T+t]\) becomes \([s,t]\), and \(T+t-\eta=t-\sigma\).  Since
\(U(\cdot,T+\sigma)=W_T(\cdot,\sigma)\), the preceding identity becomes
\[
   W_T(\cdot,t)=e^{(t-s)\Delta}W_T(\cdot,s)
   -\int_s^t e^{(t-\sigma)\Delta}\mathbb P\nabla\cdot
      (W_T(\cdot,\sigma)\otimes W_T(\cdot,\sigma))\,d\sigma,
\]
which is the mild formulation for \(W_T\).
Boundedness follows from \Cref{lem:structure-preserved}:
\[
   \|W_T(\cdot,s)\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R^3)}
   =
   \|U(\cdot,T+s)\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R^3)}
   \le
   \frac{1}{\sqrt{-T}}
   \sup_{\eta<0}\sqrt{-\eta}\,
      \|U(\cdot,\eta)\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R^3)} .
\]
Axisymmetry is unchanged because for every rotation \(R_\alpha\) around the
\(z\)-axis,
\[
   W_T(R_\alpha x,s)=U(R_\alpha x,T+s)
   =R_\alpha U(x,T+s)=R_\alpha W_T(x,s).
\]
Absence of swirl is unchanged because
\[
   (W_T)_\theta(r,z,s)=U_\theta(r,z,T+s),
\]
so \(U_\theta\equiv0\) for \(r>0\) implies \((W_T)_\theta\equiv0\) for
\(r>0\).  If \(U\) is \(L\)-periodic in \(z\), then
\[
   W_T(x_1,x_2,z+L,s)
   =U(x_1,x_2,z+L,T+s)
   =U(x_1,x_2,z,T+s)
   =W_T(x_1,x_2,z,s),
\]
so the same period works for \(W_T\).  Finally
\[
   \Gamma_{W_T}(r,z,s)=r(W_T)_\theta(r,z,s)
   =rU_\theta(r,z,T+s)=\Gamma_U(r,z,T+s).
\]
Therefore an \(L_t^\infty L_x^p\) bound for \(\Gamma_U\) on
\((-\infty,0)\) restricts to the shifted solution, since
\[
   \|\Gamma_{W_T}\|_{L_s^\infty L_x^p(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,0))}
   =
   \operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{s<0}
      \|\Gamma_U(\cdot,T+s)\|_{L^p(\mathbb R^3)}
   \le
   \|\Gamma_U\|_{L_t^\infty L_x^p(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,0))}.
\]
Similarly,
\[
   \|\Gamma_{W_T}\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,0))}
   \le
   \|\Gamma_U\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,0))}.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Spatially constant mild solutions]\label{lem:spatial-constant-mild}
Let \(W\) be a smooth mild solution of \eqref{eq:ns} on
\(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,0)\).  If there is a function
\(b:(-\infty,0)\to\mathbb R^3\) such that \(W(x,t)=b(t)\) for all
\((x,t)\), then \(b\) is constant in time.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix \(-\infty<s<t<0\).  Applying the mild formula \eqref{eq:mild-formula} to
\(W\) gives
\[
   b(t)=e^{(t-s)\Delta}b(s)
   -\int_s^t e^{(t-\sigma)\Delta}\mathbb P\nabla\cdot
      \bigl(b(\sigma)\otimes b(\sigma)\bigr)\,d\sigma .
\]
The heat semigroup leaves spatially constant vector fields fixed.  Moreover
\(b(\sigma)\otimes b(\sigma)\) is a spatially constant matrix, hence its
distributional divergence is zero.  The Duhamel integral therefore vanishes,
and \(b(t)=b(s)\).  Since \(s<t\) were arbitrary, \(b\) is constant on
\((-\infty,0)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Patching constants on backward time intervals]
\label{lem:patch-constants}
Let \(U:\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,0)\to\mathbb R^3\) be any function.  Assume
that for every \(T<0\) there exists a vector \(c_T\in\mathbb R^3\) such that
\[
   U(x,t)=c_T
   \qquad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb R^3,\quad t<T .
\]
Then there is a single vector \(c\in\mathbb R^3\) such that
\[
   U(x,t)=c
   \qquad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb R^3,\quad t<0 .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let \(T_1<T_2<0\).  The two representations hold simultaneously on the
nonempty time interval \((-\infty,T_1)\).  Hence, for any fixed
\(x_0\in\mathbb R^3\) and any \(t_0<T_1\),
\[
   c_{T_1}=U(x_0,t_0)=c_{T_2}.
\]
Thus all constants \(c_T\) are equal; denote their common value by \(c\).  If
\(t<0\), choose \(T\) with \(t<T<0\).  The hypothesis for this \(T\) gives
\(U(x,t)=c_T=c\) for every \(x\in\mathbb R^3\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Type I constants vanish]\label{lem:type-i-constant}
Let \(U\) be a smooth Type I ancient solution of \eqref{eq:ns}.  If there is a
vector \(c\in\mathbb R^3\) such that \(U(x,t)=c\) on
\(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,0)\), then \(c=0\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let
\[
   M=\sup_{t<0}\sqrt{-t}\,
      \|U(\cdot,t)\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R^3)}<\infty .
\]
For a constant solution, \(\|U(\cdot,t)\|_{L^\infty}=|c|\) for every
\(t<0\).  Hence
\[
   \sqrt{-t}\,|c|\le M\qquad\text{for every }t<0.
\]
If \(c\ne0\), the left-hand side tends to \(+\infty\) as \(t\to-\infty\),
contradicting the finiteness of \(M\).  Thus \(c=0\).
\end{proof}

\section{The axisymmetric no-swirl case}

We use the following classical Liouville theorem in the precise form needed
below.

\begin{theorem}[Koch--Nadirashvili--Seregin--Sverak]\label{thm:knss}
Let \(W\) be a bounded smooth mild ancient solution of the three-dimensional
Navier--Stokes equations on \(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,0)\).  If \(W\) is
axisymmetric without swirl, then \(W\) is constant in space and time.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
This is precisely the axisymmetric no-swirl Liouville theorem proved in
Section 5 of \cite{KNSS2009}.  Their hypothesis is stated for bounded ancient
weak solutions.  A smooth bounded mild solution satisfies the
Navier--Stokes equations distributionally and therefore is admissible for that
theorem; no additional compactness or decay assumption is used here.  If the
conclusion is expressed as a spatially constant axial drift, the mild
formulation makes the drift independent of time by
\Cref{lem:spatial-constant-mild}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:no-swirl-type-i}
Let \(U\) be a smooth Type I ancient solution of \eqref{eq:ns}.  If \(U\) is
axisymmetric without swirl, then \(U\equiv0\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix \(T<0\), and define
\[
W_T(x,s)=U(x,T+s),\qquad s<0.
\]
By \Cref{lem:time-shift}, \(W_T\) is a bounded smooth mild ancient solution on
\(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,0)\), and it is axisymmetric without swirl.
By \Cref{thm:knss}, \(W_T\) is constant on
\(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,0)\); write this constant as \(c_T\).
Equivalently,
\[
   U(x,t)=c_T\qquad\text{for }x\in\mathbb R^3,\quad t<T.
\]
Since \(T<0\) was arbitrary, \Cref{lem:patch-constants} gives a single vector
\(c\in\mathbb R^3\) such that \(U(x,t)=c\) for every \(x\in\mathbb R^3\) and
every \(t<0\).  By \Cref{lem:type-i-constant}, \(c=0\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Unconditional no-swirl Type I exclusion]
\label{cor:no-swirl-exclusion}
No nonzero Type I ancient limit obtained by the Seregin blow-up procedure from
a finite-time Type I singularity can be axisymmetric without swirl.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By the definition used in the blow-up construction, the ancient limit is a
smooth Type I ancient solution and is not identically zero: the normalization
fixes a positive pressure-normalized local critical density, as recalled in the
normalization convention above and proved in
\cite[Theorem \texttt{thm:singular-positive} and Proposition
\texttt{prop:compactness-persistence}]{Paper0LocalConcentration} and
\cite[Proposition \texttt{prop:nonzero}, Lemma \texttt{lem:smoothness}, and
Corollary \texttt{cor:classical-type-I-bound}]{Paper1TypeI}.  If this limit
were axisymmetric without swirl,
\Cref{prop:no-swirl-type-i} would imply \(U\equiv0\).  This contradicts the
nonzero normalization in \Cref{lem:nonzero-normalization}, and hence the
no-swirl alternative cannot occur.
\end{proof}

\section{Axisymmetric solutions with controlled swirl}

The angular component satisfies an equation with singular coefficients on the
axis.  Thus the no-swirl Liouville argument does not by itself treat solutions
with \(U_\theta\not\equiv0\).  We first record two unconditional Liouville
inputs from the literature.

\begin{theorem}[Lei--Zhang--Zhao]\label{thm:lzz}
Let \(W\) be a bounded smooth mild ancient solution of \eqref{eq:ns} on
\(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,0)\).  Assume that \(W\) is axisymmetric and that,
for some \(1\le p<\infty\),
\[
   rW_\theta\in L_t^\infty L_x^p
      \bigl(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,0)\bigr).
\]
Then \(W\) is constant in space and time.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The stated result is the axisymmetric ancient Liouville theorem with swirl of
Lei--Zhang--Zhao \cite{LZZ2017}.  In the form used here, the theorem assumes
that \(W\) is a bounded smooth ancient mild solution and that
\(\Gamma=rW_\theta\in L_t^\infty L_x^p\) for some \(1\le p<\infty\); its
conclusion is that \(W\) is spatially constant.  The mild formulation then
upgrades spatial constancy to constancy in time by
\Cref{lem:spatial-constant-mild}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Type I exclusion under \(L^p\)-controlled swirl]
\label{prop:lp-swirl-exclusion}
Let \(U\) be a smooth Type I ancient solution of \eqref{eq:ns}.  If \(U\) is
axisymmetric and, for some \(1\le p<\infty\),
\[
   \Gamma_U=rU_\theta\in L_t^\infty L_x^p
      \bigl(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,0)\bigr),
\]
then \(U\equiv0\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix \(T<0\) and set \(W_T(x,s)=U(x,T+s)\), \(s<0\).  By
\Cref{lem:time-shift}, \(W_T\) is a bounded smooth mild ancient solution on
\(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,0)\), it is axisymmetric, and
\[
   r(W_T)_\theta\in L_s^\infty L_x^p
      \bigl(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,0)\bigr).
\]
The hypotheses of \Cref{thm:lzz} are therefore satisfied by \(W_T\), and
\(W_T\) is a constant vector field.  Thus \(U(x,t)=c_T\) on
\(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,T)\).  Since \(T<0\) was arbitrary,
\Cref{lem:patch-constants} gives a single vector \(c\) with \(U(x,t)=c\) for
all \(x\in\mathbb R^3\) and all \(t<0\).  \Cref{lem:type-i-constant} then
gives \(c=0\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Lei--Ren--Zhang]\label{thm:lrz}
Let \(W\) be a bounded smooth mild ancient solution of \eqref{eq:ns} on
\(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,0)\).  Assume that \(W\) is axisymmetric,
periodic in the axial variable \(z\), and
\[
   rW_\theta\in L^\infty\bigl(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,0)\bigr).
\]
Then \(W\) is constant in space and time.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We use the periodic axisymmetric Liouville theorem of Lei--Ren--Zhang
\cite{LRZ2022}.  Their theorem is stated on
\(\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb T^1\); a solution on \(\mathbb R^3\) which is
periodic in \(z\) descends to the corresponding quotient as follows.  If
\(L>0\) is an axial period of the velocity, then
\[
   W(x_1,x_2,z+L,t)=W(x_1,x_2,z,t)
\]
for every \((x_1,x_2,z,t)\).  Hence the formula
\[
   \widetilde W(x_1,x_2,[z],t)=W(x_1,x_2,z,t),
   \qquad [z]\in\mathbb R/L\mathbb Z,
\]
is independent of the representative \(z\) and defines a smooth vector field on
\(\mathbb R^2\times(\mathbb R/L\mathbb Z)\).  Smoothness on the quotient is
local in the axial coordinate and follows from the smoothness and periodicity
of the Cartesian field \(W\); no separate cylindrical-coordinate argument at
the axis is required.  The pressure gradient also descends to the quotient.
Indeed, from the smooth equation
\[
   \nabla\Pi=-\partial_t W+\Delta W-W\cdot\nabla W
\]
the right-hand side is \(L\)-periodic in \(z\): spatial derivatives preserve
periodicity, and differentiating the identity
\(W(x_1,x_2,z+L,t)=W(x_1,x_2,z,t)\) in \(t\) gives periodicity of
\(\partial_tW\).  Hence \(\nabla\Pi\) is a well-defined one-form on the
quotient.  Since the Navier--Stokes system is a local differential system in
the axial variable, the descended velocity together with this descended
pressure gradient solves the same system on the quotient.  This use of the
pressure is consistent with the pressure-gauge convention above: the theorem
is applied through the velocity equation involving \(\nabla\Pi\).  To match the
period of the torus used in \cite{LRZ2022}, namely \(2\pi\), use the
Navier--Stokes scaling
\[
   W_\lambda(x,t)=\lambda W(\lambda x,\lambda^2t),\qquad
   \Pi_\lambda(x,t)=\lambda^2\Pi(\lambda x,\lambda^2t),
\]
with \(\lambda=L/(2\pi)\).  Then
\[
   W_\lambda(x_1,x_2,z+2\pi,t)
   =
   \lambda W(\lambda x_1,\lambda x_2,\lambda z+L,\lambda^2t)
   =
   W_\lambda(x_1,x_2,z,t),
\]
so the scaled solution is defined on
\(\mathbb R^2\times(\mathbb R/2\pi\mathbb Z)=\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb T^1\).
The fact that this parabolic scaling preserves the Navier--Stokes equation,
the divergence condition, and pressure gauges is the scaling statement
proved earlier in \cite[Lemmas \texttt{lem:scaling} and
\texttt{lem:gauges-harmless}]{Paper1TypeI}, together with the
scale-invariance of the pressure-normalized local quantities in
\cite[Proposition \texttt{prop:critical-scaling}]
{Paper0LocalConcentration}; we use it here only to normalize the axial period.
Axisymmetry is preserved because scalar dilations commute with rotations around
the \(z\)-axis.  Boundedness is preserved because
\[
   \|W_\lambda\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,0))}
   =
   \lambda
   \|W\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,0))}<\infty .
\]
Ancientness is preserved because \(t<0\) if and only if
\(\lambda^2t<0\).  The mild formulation is preserved by applying the same
change of variables to the Duhamel formula.  Equivalently, the scaled pair is a
smooth solution of the differential Navier--Stokes system on every finite time
interval and therefore has the standard mild representation on that interval
\cite{Kato1984,Sohr2001}.  Finally, the scaling preserves
boundedness of the swirl variable because
\[
   r(W_\lambda)_\theta(r,z,t)
   =
   r\lambda W_\theta(\lambda r,\lambda z,\lambda^2t)
   =
   (\lambda r)W_\theta(\lambda r,\lambda z,\lambda^2t).
\]
All hypotheses of the Lei--Ren--Zhang theorem are therefore satisfied on
\(\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb T^1\).  Their conclusion gives an axial constant
field, or equivalently a spatially constant drift.  By
\Cref{lem:spatial-constant-mild} this drift is independent of time.  Thus, if
\(W_\lambda\equiv c_\lambda\), then for every \(x\) and \(t\),
\[
   W(x,t)=\lambda^{-1}W_\lambda(\lambda^{-1}x,\lambda^{-2}t)
   =\lambda^{-1}c_\lambda .
\]
Thus the original lifted solution \(W\) is also constant on
\(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,0)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Type I exclusion under periodic bounded swirl]
\label{prop:periodic-swirl-exclusion}
Let \(U\) be a smooth Type I ancient solution of \eqref{eq:ns}.  If \(U\) is
axisymmetric, periodic in \(z\), and
\[
   \Gamma_U=rU_\theta\in L^\infty\bigl(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,0)\bigr),
\]
then \(U\equiv0\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix \(T<0\) and set \(W_T(x,s)=U(x,T+s)\), \(s<0\).  By
\Cref{lem:time-shift}, \(W_T\) is a bounded smooth mild ancient solution,
axisymmetric, periodic in \(z\), and with
\[
   r(W_T)_\theta\in L^\infty
      \bigl(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,0)\bigr).
\]
Applying \Cref{thm:lrz} gives that \(W_T\) is constant on
\(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,0)\); write the corresponding constant as
\(c_T\).  Therefore
\[
   U(x,t)=c_T\qquad\text{for }x\in\mathbb R^3,\quad t<T .
\]
Since \(T<0\) was arbitrary,
\Cref{lem:patch-constants} yields a global constant vector \(c\) on
\(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,0)\).  By \Cref{lem:type-i-constant}, \(c=0\).
\end{proof}

The remaining controlled-swirl case is not covered by the preceding imported
Liouville theorems.  We isolate the exact fixed-exponent Liouville input needed
for this case.

\begin{hypothesis}[Liouville theorem with controlled swirl]\label{hyp:swirl}
Fix \(\gamma>0\).  Let \(U\) be a smooth Type I ancient solution of
\eqref{eq:ns}.  Assume that \(U\) is axisymmetric and that its velocity \(V\) in
self-similar variables satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:controlled-swirl-assumption}
\sup_{\tau\in\mathbb R}\sup_{\rho>0,\ Z\in\mathbb R}
\rho^\gamma |V_\theta(\rho,Z,\tau)|<\infty.
\end{equation}
Then \(U\equiv0\).
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:swirl-exponent}
The exponent \(\gamma\) is part of \Cref{hyp:swirl}.  The result is conditional
at this value of \(\gamma\) and makes no assertion at other exponents.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:swirl-exclusion}
Fix \(\gamma>0\), and suppose that the Liouville hypothesis
\Cref{hyp:swirl} holds for this value of \(\gamma\).  Let \(U\) be a nonzero
Type I ancient limit arising from a finite-time Type I singularity.  Then \(U\)
cannot be axisymmetric with self-similar velocity satisfying
\eqref{eq:controlled-swirl-assumption}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The parameter in the proposition is the same \(\gamma\) as in
\Cref{hyp:swirl}.  By the blow-up construction cited in the normalization
convention, specifically
\cite[Proposition \texttt{prop:nonzero}, Lemma \texttt{lem:smoothness}, and
Corollary \texttt{cor:classical-type-I-bound}]{Paper1TypeI}, \(U\) is a smooth
Type I ancient solution and is not identically zero.  The additional
hypotheses in the proposition are precisely axisymmetry and
\eqref{eq:controlled-swirl-assumption}.  Thus all hypotheses of
\Cref{hyp:swirl} are satisfied with the same exponent \(\gamma\), and no
decay, periodicity, or integrability condition beyond those stated in
\Cref{hyp:swirl} is used.  Hence
\Cref{hyp:swirl} gives \(U\equiv0\).  This contradicts the nonzero
normalization of the Type I ancient limit in
\Cref{lem:nonzero-normalization}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:swirl-scale}
In physical variables, \eqref{eq:controlled-swirl-assumption} is equivalent to
\[
\sup_{t<0}\sup_{r>0,\ z\in\mathbb R}
(-t)^{(1-\gamma)/2}r^\gamma |U_\theta(r,z,t)|<\infty .
\]
Indeed, \(r=\sqrt{-t}\rho\) and
\(V_\theta(\rho,Z,\tau)=\sqrt{-t}\,U_\theta(r,z,t)\).  Therefore
\[
   \rho^\gamma |V_\theta(\rho,Z,\tau)|
   =
   \left(\frac{r}{\sqrt{-t}}\right)^\gamma
   \sqrt{-t}\,|U_\theta(r,z,t)|
   =
   (-t)^{(1-\gamma)/2}r^\gamma |U_\theta(r,z,t)|.
\]
The change of variables \((r,z,t)\leftrightarrow(\rho,Z,\tau)\) is bijective
between \(\{r>0,z\in\mathbb R,t<0\}\) and
\(\{\rho>0,Z\in\mathbb R,\tau\in\mathbb R\}\), so the two suprema are equal.
Thus \eqref{eq:controlled-swirl-assumption} is equivalent to the displayed
scale-invariant physical-variable bound.  We state \Cref{hyp:swirl} in
self-similar variables so that the geometric cylindrical weight and the Type I
time weight are separated.
\end{remark}

\section{Fast decay in self-similar variables}

We next formulate the spatial decay case.  Let \(V\) be the velocity in
self-similar variables associated with a Type I ancient solution.  For \(m>0\)
consider the weighted condition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-decay}
\sup_{\tau\in\mathbb R}
\int_{\mathbb R^3}(1+|y|^2)^m |V(y,\tau)|^2\,dy<\infty .
\end{equation}
Let
\[
   A_m=\sup_{\tau\in\mathbb R}
   \int_{\mathbb R^3}(1+|y|^2)^m |V(y,\tau)|^2\,dy .
\]
Then \eqref{eq:weighted-decay} implies uniform local \(L^2\) control.  Indeed,
since \(m>0\), the weight satisfies
\((1+|y|^2)^m\ge1\) for every \(y\).  Hence, for every \(R<\infty\) and every
\(\tau\),
\[
   \int_{B_R}|V(y,\tau)|^2\,dy
   \le
   \int_{\mathbb R^3}(1+|y|^2)^m |V(y,\tau)|^2\,dy .
\]
Taking the supremum over \(\tau\) gives
\[
   \sup_{\tau\in\mathbb R}\int_{B_R}|V(y,\tau)|^2\,dy
   \le A_m .
\]
It also rules out spatial escape of \(L^2\) mass in self-similar variables:
for every \(R>0\), the implication \(|y|>R\) gives
\((1+|y|^2)^m\ge(1+R^2)^m\).  Therefore, for every \(\tau\),
\[
   \int_{|y|>R}|V(y,\tau)|^2\,dy
   \le
   (1+R^2)^{-m}
   \int_{\mathbb R^3}(1+|y|^2)^m |V(y,\tau)|^2\,dy .
\]
Taking the supremum over \(\tau\) gives
\[
   \sup_{\tau\in\mathbb R}\int_{|y|>R}|V(y,\tau)|^2\,dy
   \le (1+R^2)^{-m}A_m .
\]
Thus the right-hand side tends to zero as \(R\to\infty\).  The present paper
does not use any integration by parts or backward-uniqueness argument beyond
the fixed-weight Liouville input stated next.

\begin{hypothesis}[Weighted Liouville theorem]\label{hyp:weighted}
Fix \(m>0\).  Let \(U\) be a smooth Type I ancient solution of \eqref{eq:ns}.
Assume that its velocity \(V\) in self-similar variables satisfies
\eqref{eq:weighted-decay}.  Then \(U\equiv0\).
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:weighted-exponent}
The weight \(m\) is part of \Cref{hyp:weighted}.  The result is conditional at
this value of \(m\) and makes no assertion at other weights.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:weighted-exclusion}
Fix \(m>0\), and suppose that the Liouville hypothesis \Cref{hyp:weighted}
holds for this value of \(m\).  Let \(U\) be a nonzero Type I ancient limit
arising from a finite-time Type I singularity.  Then its velocity in
self-similar variables cannot satisfy \eqref{eq:weighted-decay}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The parameter in the proposition is the same \(m\) as in \Cref{hyp:weighted}.
By the blow-up construction cited in the normalization convention, specifically
\cite[Proposition \texttt{prop:nonzero}, Lemma \texttt{lem:smoothness}, and
Corollary \texttt{cor:classical-type-I-bound}]{Paper1TypeI}, \(U\) is a smooth
Type I ancient solution and is not identically zero.  If its
self-similar velocity \(V\) satisfied \eqref{eq:weighted-decay}, then all
hypotheses of \Cref{hyp:weighted} would be satisfied with the same weight
\(m\).  Therefore \(U\equiv0\), contradicting the nonzero normalization
inherited from the singular blow-up sequence, as stated in
\Cref{lem:nonzero-normalization}.
\end{proof}

\section{The Type I exclusion under symmetry and decay}

The preceding sections give the following exclusion statement.  The no-swirl
alternative, the \(L^p\)-controlled swirl alternative, and the periodic
bounded-\(\Gamma\) alternative are consequences of published Liouville
theorems.  The pointwise controlled-swirl and decay alternatives are
conditional on the corresponding fixed-exponent Liouville hypotheses stated
above.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:symmetry-decay-exclusion}
Let \(U\) be a nonzero Type I ancient limit arising from a finite-time Type I
singularity of the three-dimensional Navier--Stokes equations, and let \(V\) be
its velocity in self-similar variables.  Then \(U\) is not axisymmetric without
swirl.  Moreover, if \(U\) is axisymmetric, then neither of the following
additional alternatives can occur:
\[
   rU_\theta\in L_t^\infty L_x^p
      \bigl(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,0)\bigr)
      \quad\hbox{for some }1\le p<\infty,
\]
or \(U\) is periodic in \(z\) and
\[
   rU_\theta\in L^\infty\bigl(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,0)\bigr).
\]
Finally, fix \(\gamma>0\) and suppose that the Liouville hypothesis in
\Cref{hyp:swirl} holds.  Then \(U\) cannot be axisymmetric and satisfy
\eqref{eq:controlled-swirl-assumption}.  Likewise, fix \(m>0\) and suppose that
the Liouville hypothesis in \Cref{hyp:weighted} holds.  Then \(V\) cannot satisfy
\eqref{eq:weighted-decay}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose first that \(U\) were axisymmetric without swirl.  Since \(U\) is a
nonzero Type I ancient limit obtained from the blow-up procedure, it is in the
class covered by \Cref{cor:no-swirl-exclusion}.  That corollary excludes
precisely such limits under the no-swirl hypothesis.  This gives a
contradiction, and hence the no-swirl alternative cannot occur.

Assume next that \(U\) is axisymmetric and that
\(\Gamma_U=rU_\theta\in L_t^\infty L_x^p\) for some \(1\le p<\infty\).
The preceding paragraph and the companion-paper normalization give the PDE
properties needed to use \Cref{prop:lp-swirl-exclusion}: \(U\) is a smooth
Type I ancient solution, it is axisymmetric, and the displayed
\(L_t^\infty L_x^p\)-bound is exactly the integrability hypothesis in that
proposition.  Hence \Cref{prop:lp-swirl-exclusion} gives \(U\equiv0\).  This
contradicts the nonzero normalization in \Cref{lem:nonzero-normalization}.

If \(U\) is periodic in \(z\) and \(\Gamma_U\in L^\infty\), then the
hypotheses of \Cref{prop:periodic-swirl-exclusion} are likewise exactly the
smooth Type I ancient property, axisymmetry, axial periodicity, and boundedness
of \(rU_\theta\).  Therefore \Cref{prop:periodic-swirl-exclusion} gives
\(U\equiv0\), again contradicting \Cref{lem:nonzero-normalization}.

Now fix \(\gamma>0\) and assume \Cref{hyp:swirl}.  If the displayed
pointwise self-similar swirl bound held, then \(U\) would be a nonzero Type I
ancient limit which is axisymmetric and satisfies
\eqref{eq:controlled-swirl-assumption} with the same exponent \(\gamma\).
These are precisely the hypotheses of \Cref{prop:swirl-exclusion}, including
the fixed value of \(\gamma\).  That proposition says that no such limit
exists, a contradiction.

Finally, fix \(m>0\) and assume \Cref{hyp:weighted}.  If
\eqref{eq:weighted-decay} held, then \(U\) would be a nonzero Type I ancient
limit whose self-similar velocity satisfies the weighted condition with the
same exponent \(m\).  These are precisely the hypotheses of
\Cref{prop:weighted-exclusion}.  That proposition excludes such a limit, again
giving a contradiction.  These contradictions prove all alternatives stated in
the theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:relation-to-previous}
The no-swirl case is an unconditional exclusion in the Type I blow-up
analysis.  The \(L_t^\infty L_x^p\)-controlled swirl case and the
\(z\)-periodic bounded-\(\Gamma\) case are also excluded by existing Liouville
theorems.  In the pointwise controlled-swirl and weighted cases, the only
additional input is the corresponding fixed-exponent Liouville theorem.
\end{remark}
