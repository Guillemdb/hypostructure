\title[Ancient Navier--Stokes solutions under symmetry and decay]{Ancient Navier--Stokes solutions under axisymmetry and decay}
\author{Guillem Duran Ballester}
\date{}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
We consider ancient solutions of the three-dimensional Navier--Stokes equations
arising in the Type I blow-up analysis under three additional structures:
axisymmetry without swirl, axisymmetry with a quantitative bound on the angular
velocity, and weighted spatial decay in self-similar variables.  In the no-swirl
case the Liouville theorem of Koch--Nadirashvili--Seregin--Sverak, applied after
a time shift, implies that every smooth Type I ancient solution is identically
zero.  We also import two established swirl-bearing axisymmetric Liouville
theorems: the Lei--Zhang--Zhao theorem for
\(\Gamma=rU_\theta\in L_t^\infty L_x^p\), \(1\le p<\infty\), and the
Lei--Ren--Zhang theorem for \(z\)-periodic ancient solutions with bounded
\(\Gamma\).  For the remaining pointwise controlled-swirl and weighted cases
we state fixed-exponent Liouville assumptions and prove the corresponding
Type I exclusions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Let \(U:\mathbb R^{3}\times(-\infty,0)\to\mathbb R^{3}\) and
\(P:\mathbb R^{3}\times(-\infty,0)\to\mathbb R\) solve
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns}
\partial_t U-\Delta U+U\cdot\nabla U+\nabla P=0,\qquad
\nabla\cdot U=0.
\end{equation}
The Type I analysis of a finite-time singularity leads, after rescaling about a
singular point, to nonzero mild ancient solutions satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:type-i}
\sup_{t<0}\sqrt{-t}\,\|U(\cdot,t)\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R^3)}<\infty .
\end{equation}
Here and below, a nonzero ancient limit is understood in the usual blow-up
sense: the rescalings have been normalized so that the limiting solution is not
identically zero, for instance through a positive pressure-normalized local
critical density.  The present paper records three additional structures under
which such limits can be excluded.

The first case is axisymmetry without swirl.  In this case the argument is
settled by an existing Liouville theorem: bounded ancient axisymmetric no-swirl
solutions are constant.  Since a solution satisfying \eqref{eq:type-i} is bounded
on every time interval \((-\infty,T]\), \(T<0\), this theorem applies after a
time shift.  The Type I decay as \(t\to-\infty\) then eliminates the constant.

The second case is axisymmetry with swirl satisfying a quantitative bound.
The available estimates for the angular component are substantially weaker than
in the no-swirl case.  Two subbranches, however, are already covered by
published Liouville theorems: finite \(L^p\) control of the swirl variable
\(\Gamma=rU_\theta\), and axial periodicity with bounded \(\Gamma\).  After
recording these imports, we state the remaining pointwise controlled-swirl
criterion as a fixed-exponent assumption, with \(\gamma>0\) fixed throughout
the statement.

The third case is uniform spatial decay in self-similar variables.  This
condition rules out spatial escape in weighted \(L^2\), but it does not by
itself furnish a complete time-dependent Liouville theorem.  We therefore state
the weighted Liouville assumption used below and prove the reduction from that
assumption.

\subsection{Normalization convention}

Every exclusion below is applied to an ancient limit produced by a singularity.
Such a limit is not merely a bounded ancient solution: it carries a fixed
positive pressure-normalized local critical density inherited from the
blow-up sequence.  Therefore any Liouville theorem which concludes
\(U\equiv0\) contradicts the construction of the limit.  This is the only
place where the singular origin of the ancient solution is used in this paper.

\section{Ancient Type I solutions and self-similar variables}

\begin{definition}\label{def:type-i-ancient}
A smooth mild solution \(U\) of \eqref{eq:ns} on
\(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,0)\) is called a Type I ancient solution if
\eqref{eq:type-i} holds.  Here mild is understood in the standard velocity
formulation, equivalently by the Duhamel formula on each interval
\([a,b]\subset(-\infty,0)\).  The velocity and pressure in backward
self-similar variables are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:self-similar}
V(y,\tau)=\sqrt{-t}\,U(x,t),\qquad
Q(y,\tau)=(-t)P(x,t),\qquad
y=\frac{x}{\sqrt{-t}},\qquad
\tau=-\log(-t).
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Self-similar equation]\label{lem:self-similar-equation}
Let \((U,P)\) be a smooth solution of \eqref{eq:ns}, and define \((V,Q)\) by
\eqref{eq:self-similar}.  Then \(V\) is defined on
\(\mathbb R^3\times\mathbb R\) and satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:leray}
\partial_\tau V-\Delta V+\frac12 y\cdot\nabla V+\frac12 V
 +V\cdot\nabla V+\nabla Q=0,\qquad \nabla\cdot V=0.
\end{equation}
Conversely, every smooth solution \((V,Q)\) of \eqref{eq:leray} determines a
smooth solution of \eqref{eq:ns} by the inverse change of variables.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write
\[
\lambda(t)=(-t)^{-1/2},\qquad y=\lambda(t)x,\qquad
\tau=-\log(-t),
\]
so that \(U(x,t)=\lambda(t)V(y,\tau)\) and
\(P(x,t)=\lambda(t)^2Q(y,\tau)\).  Since
\[
\lambda'(t)=\frac12\lambda(t)^3,\qquad
\tau'(t)=\lambda(t)^2,\qquad
\partial_t y=\frac12\lambda(t)^2y,
\]
one has
\[
\partial_t U
=\lambda^3\left(\partial_\tau V+\frac12 y\cdot\nabla V+\frac12 V\right),
\qquad
\Delta_x U=\lambda^3\Delta_y V,
\]
and
\[
U\cdot\nabla_x U=\lambda^3V\cdot\nabla_y V,\qquad
\nabla_x P=\lambda^3\nabla_y Q.
\]
Dividing the Navier--Stokes equation by \(\lambda^3\) gives
\eqref{eq:leray}.  The divergence condition transforms as
\(\nabla_x\cdot U=\lambda^2\nabla_y\cdot V\).  The inverse computation is the
same calculation read backwards.
\end{proof}

The Type I bound is exactly the uniform \(L^\infty\) bound for \(V\):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:type-i-v}
\sup_{\tau\in\mathbb R}\|V(\cdot,\tau)\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R^3)}<\infty .
\end{equation}
Indeed, if \(t=-e^{-\tau}\), then
\[
\|V(\cdot,\tau)\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R^3)}
=\sqrt{-t}\,\|U(\cdot,t)\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R^3)}.
\]

For axisymmetric flows we write
\[
x=(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta,z),\qquad
U=U_r e_r+U_\theta e_\theta+U_z e_z.
\]
The flow is said to have no swirl if \(U_\theta\equiv0\).  The same convention is
used for \(V\), with cylindrical coordinates
\[
y=(\rho\cos\theta,\rho\sin\theta,Z).
\]
We write
\[
   \Gamma_U(r,z,t)=rU_\theta(r,z,t),\qquad
   \Gamma_V(\rho,Z,\tau)=\rho V_\theta(\rho,Z,\tau).
\]
Under the self-similar change of variables these quantities agree:
\(\Gamma_V(\rho,Z,\tau)=\Gamma_U(r,z,t)\), where
\((r,z)=\sqrt{-t}\,(\rho,Z)\).

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:structure-preserved}
Axisymmetry and the no-swirl condition are preserved by the self-similar change
of variables \eqref{eq:self-similar}.  The bounds
\begin{equation}\label{eq:swirl-control}
\sup_{\tau\in\mathbb R}\sup_{\rho>0,\ Z\in\mathbb R}
\rho^\gamma |V_\theta(\rho,Z,\tau)|<\infty
\end{equation}
are invariant under translations of \(\tau\).
Moreover, if \(U\) satisfies \eqref{eq:type-i}, then for every \(T<0\) the
restriction of \(U\) to \(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,T]\) is bounded.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The self-similar change of variables is a scalar dilation in space together with
a scalar multiplication of the velocity.  It therefore commutes with rotations
around the \(z\)-axis and preserves the cylindrical decomposition of the vector
field.  This proves the assertions on axisymmetry and no swirl.  The expression
\(\rho^\gamma |V_\theta|\) is written directly in self-similar coordinates, so
\eqref{eq:swirl-control} is unchanged when \(V(y,\tau)\) is replaced by
\(V(y,\tau+a)\), \(a\in\mathbb R\).  Finally, if \(t\le T<0\), then
\eqref{eq:type-i} gives
\[
\|U(\cdot,t)\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R^3)}
\le
\frac{1}{\sqrt{-T}}
\sup_{s<0}\sqrt{-s}\,\|U(\cdot,s)\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R^3)} .
\]
\end{proof}

\section{The axisymmetric no-swirl case}

We use the following classical Liouville theorem in the precise form needed
below.

\begin{theorem}[Koch--Nadirashvili--Seregin--Sverak]\label{thm:knss}
Let \(W\) be a bounded smooth mild ancient solution of the three-dimensional
Navier--Stokes equations on \(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,0)\).  If \(W\) is
axisymmetric without swirl, then \(W\) is constant in space and time.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
This is the axisymmetric no-swirl Liouville theorem proved in
\cite{KNSS2009}, specialized to smooth solutions.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:no-swirl-type-i}
Let \(U\) be a smooth Type I ancient solution of \eqref{eq:ns}.  If \(U\) is
axisymmetric without swirl, then \(U\equiv0\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let
\[
M=\sup_{t<0}\sqrt{-t}\,\|U(\cdot,t)\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R^3)}.
\]
Fix \(T<0\), and define
\[
W_T(x,s)=U(x,T+s),\qquad s<0.
\]
Then \(W_T\) is a smooth mild ancient solution, axisymmetric without swirl, and
\[
\|W_T(\cdot,s)\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R^3)}
\le \frac{M}{\sqrt{-(T+s)}}\le \frac{M}{\sqrt{-T}},
\qquad s<0.
\]
By \Cref{thm:knss}, \(W_T\) is constant on
\(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,0)\).  Hence \(U\) is constant on
\(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,T)\).  If \(T_1<T_2<0\), the constants obtained
on \((-\infty,T_1)\) and \((-\infty,T_2)\) agree on the overlap.  Since
\(T<0\) was arbitrary, there is a single vector \(c\in\mathbb R^3\) such that
\(U(x,t)=c\) for all \(x\in\mathbb R^3\) and \(t<0\).  The Type I bound gives
\[
|c|\le \frac{M}{\sqrt{-t}}\qquad\text{for every }t<0.
\]
Letting \(t\to-\infty\) yields \(c=0\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Unconditional no-swirl Type I exclusion]
\label{cor:no-swirl-exclusion}
No nonzero Type I ancient limit obtained by the Seregin blow-up procedure from
a finite-time Type I singularity can be axisymmetric without swirl.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Such a limit satisfies \eqref{eq:type-i}.  If it were axisymmetric without
swirl, \Cref{prop:no-swirl-type-i} would give \(U\equiv0\), contradicting the
nonzero normalization of the blow-up limit.
\end{proof}

\section{Axisymmetric solutions with controlled swirl}

The angular component satisfies an equation with singular coefficients on the
axis.  This prevents the preceding no-swirl argument from closing once
\(U_\theta\) is nonzero.  We first record two unconditional literature imports
which close useful swirl-bearing subbranches.

\begin{theorem}[Lei--Zhang--Zhao]\label{thm:lzz}
Let \(W\) be a bounded smooth mild ancient solution of \eqref{eq:ns} on
\(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,0)\).  Assume that \(W\) is axisymmetric and that,
for some \(1\le p<\infty\),
\[
   rW_\theta\in L_t^\infty L_x^p
      \bigl(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,0)\bigr).
\]
Then \(W\) is constant in space and time.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
This is the swirl-bearing ancient Liouville theorem of Lei--Zhang--Zhao
\cite{LZZ2017}, specialized to bounded smooth mild ancient solutions.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Type I exclusion under \(L^p\)-controlled swirl]
\label{prop:lp-swirl-exclusion}
Let \(U\) be a smooth Type I ancient solution of \eqref{eq:ns}.  If \(U\) is
axisymmetric and, for some \(1\le p<\infty\),
\[
   \Gamma_U=rU_\theta\in L_t^\infty L_x^p
      \bigl(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,0)\bigr),
\]
then \(U\equiv0\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\Cref{thm:lzz} gives that \(U\) is a constant vector.  The Type I bound
\eqref{eq:type-i} eliminates this constant exactly as in
\Cref{prop:no-swirl-type-i}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Lei--Ren--Zhang]\label{thm:lrz}
Let \(W\) be a bounded smooth mild ancient solution of \eqref{eq:ns} on
\(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,0)\).  Assume that \(W\) is axisymmetric,
periodic in the axial variable \(z\), and
\[
   rW_\theta\in L^\infty\bigl(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,0)\bigr).
\]
Then \(W\) is constant in space and time.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
This is the periodic axisymmetric Liouville theorem of Lei--Ren--Zhang
\cite{LRZ2022}.  Their theorem is stated on
\(\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb T^1\); a solution on \(\mathbb R^3\) which is
periodic in \(z\) descends to that quotient, after the harmless Navier--Stokes
scaling needed to normalize the period.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Type I exclusion under periodic bounded swirl]
\label{prop:periodic-swirl-exclusion}
Let \(U\) be a smooth Type I ancient solution of \eqref{eq:ns}.  If \(U\) is
axisymmetric, periodic in \(z\), and
\[
   \Gamma_U=rU_\theta\in L^\infty\bigl(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,0)\bigr),
\]
then \(U\equiv0\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\Cref{thm:lrz} gives that \(U\) is constant.  The Type I bound
\eqref{eq:type-i} then forces that constant to vanish.
\end{proof}

The remaining controlled-swirl branch below is not covered by these imports.
The following fixed-exponent Liouville theorem is the assumption used for that
branch.

\begin{assumption}[Liouville theorem with controlled swirl]\label{ass:swirl}
Fix \(\gamma>0\).  Let \(U\) be a smooth Type I ancient solution of
\eqref{eq:ns}.  Assume that \(U\) is axisymmetric and that its velocity \(V\) in
self-similar variables satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:controlled-swirl-assumption}
\sup_{\tau\in\mathbb R}\sup_{\rho>0,\ Z\in\mathbb R}
\rho^\gamma |V_\theta(\rho,Z,\tau)|<\infty.
\end{equation}
Then \(U\equiv0\).
\end{assumption}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:swirl-exponent}
The exponent \(\gamma\) is part of \Cref{ass:swirl}.  The result is conditional
at this value of \(\gamma\) and makes no assertion at other exponents.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:swirl-exclusion}
Fix \(\gamma>0\), and assume the Liouville theorem in \Cref{ass:swirl} for this
value of \(\gamma\).  Let \(U\) be a nonzero Type I ancient limit arising from a
finite-time Type I singularity.  If \(U\) is axisymmetric and its velocity in
self-similar variables satisfies \eqref{eq:controlled-swirl-assumption}, then
no such \(U\) exists.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The hypotheses place \(U\) in the class covered by \Cref{ass:swirl}.  Hence
\(U\equiv0\), contrary to the nonzero normalization of the ancient limit.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:swirl-scale}
In physical variables, \eqref{eq:controlled-swirl-assumption} is equivalent to
\[
\sup_{t<0}\sup_{r>0,\ z\in\mathbb R}
(-t)^{(1-\gamma)/2}r^\gamma |U_\theta(r,z,t)|<\infty .
\]
This is the scale-invariant form of the condition.  It is written in
self-similar variables in \Cref{ass:swirl} to avoid mixing the geometric
cylindrical variables with the Type I time weight.
\end{remark}

\section{Fast decay in self-similar variables}

We next formulate the spatial decay case.  Let \(V\) be the velocity in
self-similar variables associated with a Type I ancient solution.  For \(m>0\)
consider the weighted condition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-decay}
\sup_{\tau\in\mathbb R}
\int_{\mathbb R^3}(1+|y|^2)^m |V(y,\tau)|^2\,dy<\infty .
\end{equation}
This condition is stronger than local \(L^2\) boundedness; it rules out spatial
escape of \(L^2\) mass in self-similar variables.  Any integration by parts or
backward-uniqueness argument required to exploit this decay is contained in the
fixed-weight Liouville theorem stated below.

\begin{assumption}[Weighted Liouville theorem]\label{ass:weighted}
Fix \(m>0\).  Let \(U\) be a smooth Type I ancient solution of \eqref{eq:ns}.
Assume that its velocity \(V\) in self-similar variables satisfies
\eqref{eq:weighted-decay}.  Then \(U\equiv0\).
\end{assumption}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:weighted-exponent}
The weight \(m\) is part of \Cref{ass:weighted}.  The result is conditional at
this value of \(m\) and makes no assertion at other weights.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:weighted-exclusion}
Fix \(m>0\), and assume the Liouville theorem in \Cref{ass:weighted} for this
value of \(m\).  Let \(U\) be a nonzero Type I ancient limit arising from a
finite-time Type I singularity.  If its velocity in self-similar variables
satisfies \eqref{eq:weighted-decay}, then no such \(U\) exists.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The hypotheses put \(U\) in the class covered by \Cref{ass:weighted}.  Hence
\(U\equiv0\), contradicting the nonzero normalization of the ancient limit.
\end{proof}

\section{The Type I exclusion under symmetry and decay}

The preceding sections assemble into a single exclusion statement.  The no-swirl
alternative, the \(L^p\)-controlled swirl alternative, and the periodic
bounded-\(\Gamma\) alternative are unconditional literature consequences.  The
pointwise controlled-swirl and decay alternatives are conditional on the
corresponding fixed-exponent Liouville theorems.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:symmetry-decay-exclusion}
Let \(U\) be a nonzero Type I ancient limit arising from a finite-time Type I
singularity of the three-dimensional Navier--Stokes equations, and let \(V\) be
its velocity in self-similar variables.  Then \(U\) is not axisymmetric without
swirl.  Moreover, if \(U\) is axisymmetric, then neither of the following
additional alternatives can occur:
\[
   rU_\theta\in L_t^\infty L_x^p
      \bigl(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,0)\bigr)
      \quad\hbox{for some }1\le p<\infty,
\]
or \(U\) is periodic in \(z\) and
\[
   rU_\theta\in L^\infty\bigl(\mathbb R^3\times(-\infty,0)\bigr).
\]
Finally, fix \(\gamma>0\) and assume the Liouville theorem in
\Cref{ass:swirl}.  Then \(U\) cannot be axisymmetric and satisfy
\eqref{eq:controlled-swirl-assumption}.  Likewise, fix \(m>0\) and assume the
Liouville theorem in \Cref{ass:weighted}.  Then \(V\) cannot satisfy
\eqref{eq:weighted-decay}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The no-swirl assertion is \Cref{cor:no-swirl-exclusion}.  The assertion under
\(L^p\)-controlled swirl is \Cref{prop:lp-swirl-exclusion}, and the assertion
under periodic bounded swirl is \Cref{prop:periodic-swirl-exclusion}.  The
conditional pointwise controlled-swirl assertion is
\Cref{prop:swirl-exclusion}.  The assertion under weighted decay is
\Cref{prop:weighted-exclusion}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:relation-to-previous}
The no-swirl case is an unconditional closed branch of the Type I blow-up
analysis.  The \(L_t^\infty L_x^p\)-controlled swirl branch and the
\(z\)-periodic bounded-\(\Gamma\) branch are also closed by existing
Liouville theorems.  In the remaining pointwise controlled-swirl and weighted
cases, the remaining work is exactly the proof of the corresponding
fixed-exponent Liouville theorem.
\end{remark}
